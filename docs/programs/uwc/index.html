THE BIG PICTURE

About the program

UWC is a global movement of schools and colleges that uses education to bring young people across cultures, countries and backgrounds. Its two-year International Baccalaureate route combines demanding academics with service, outdoor learning, creativity and shared residential life. Students apply to the movement, often through a volunteer national committee, and may be placed at different UWC schools according to availability and selection.

The purpose is larger than a diploma. UWC asks students to listen to perspectives unlike their own and turn what they learn into action for peace and a sustainable future. Daily life is intentionally international: lessons, meals, shared rooms, activities and community work become places to practice cooperation.

Applicants should pay close attention to their route. National committees set local deadlines and assess students in context. More than 80% of students selected through national committees receive financial support based on need, while the Global Selection Programme is a full-fee route. The official application guide explains which route fits each applicant.

THE JOURNEY

How it came to life

A brief look at the moments that shaped UWC.

1955

An idea shaped by cooperation

Educational pioneer Kurt Hahn was inspired by people who had recently been on opposite sides of war working together. He imagined a school where young people could build understanding across borders.

1962

The first college opens

UWC Atlantic admitted its first students in Wales, starting the international education movement.

1970

National committees take shape

Volunteer committees began selecting students locally and widening access to UWC places.

Today

A global network

UWC reports 18 schools and colleges and more than 85,000 alumni from over 180 countries since 1962.
